Understanding THC & CBD A Beginner’s Guide To Cannabinoids

If you’re new to cannabis, you’ve probably noticed two letters appearing on almost every product label:

THC.

CBD.

These are two of the best-known cannabinoids found in cannabis, but they aren’t the same.

Understanding the difference between THC and CBD can make it easier to read product labels, compare products, and make more informed purchasing decisions.

Let’s break down the basics.

What Are Cannabinoids?

Cannabinoids are naturally occurring compounds found in cannabis.

Cannabis contains many different cannabinoids, but THC and CBD are among the most commonly discussed.

Different cannabinoids interact with the body’s endocannabinoid system in different ways, and cannabis products can contain varying combinations and concentrations of these compounds.

That’s why looking beyond the product name can be useful when comparing cannabis products.

What Is THC?

THC stands for tetrahydrocannabinol.

When people talk about the intoxicating effects of cannabis, they’re usually referring primarily to THC.

THC can produce effects such as:

  • Euphoria
  • Relaxation
  • Altered perception
  • Changes in time perception
  • Impaired coordination
  • Changes in attention and concentration

Because THC can impair coordination and judgment, consuming THC can affect activities such as driving.

Never drive or operate machinery while impaired.

What Is CBD?

CBD stands for cannabidiol.

CBD is another naturally occurring cannabinoid found in cannabis.

Unlike THC, CBD is not generally intoxicating and doesn’t produce the same type of high commonly associated with THC.

CBD products can be found in many different formats and may contain CBD by itself or alongside THC.

THC vs. CBD: The Basic Difference

A simple way to remember the distinction is:

THC

  • Intoxicating
  • Associated with the cannabis high
  • Can affect coordination and perception
  • Commonly listed as a percentage in flower
  • Often listed in milligrams for edibles

CBD

  • Not generally intoxicating
  • Found naturally in cannabis
  • Can be used alone or alongside THC
  • Commonly listed in milligrams or percentages depending on the product

However, the effects of a cannabis product aren’t determined by THC or CBD alone.

What Is THC Percentage?

If you’ve browsed cannabis flower, you’ve probably seen numbers such as:

18% THC

22% THC

27% THC

This percentage represents the concentration of THC in the product.

A higher percentage generally means a greater concentration of THC, but that doesn’t automatically mean the product will be a better choice for everyone.

Other factors matter too.

What Does “Total THC” Mean?

Product labels can contain several cannabinoid measurements.

You may see:

  • THC
  • THCA
  • Total THC
  • CBD
  • CBDA
  • Total CBD

These measurements can sometimes be confusing.

THCA is a cannabinoid that can convert into THC when exposed to heat.

Because of this, cannabis flower may contain a significant amount of THCA even when the amount of active THC listed separately is lower.

When comparing products, read the entire cannabinoid section rather than focusing on a single number.

What About CBD Ratios?

Some products contain both THC and CBD.

You may see descriptions such as:

1:1 THC:CBD

This generally indicates a product containing comparable amounts of THC and CBD.

Other products may have a higher THC concentration relative to CBD, or vice versa.

The cannabinoid ratio can be useful information when comparing products.

Does More THC Mean Better Cannabis?

Not necessarily.

It’s easy to assume that a product with the highest THC percentage must automatically be the best option.

But cannabis experiences are more complicated than a single number.

Two products with similar THC percentages can still differ in:

  • Aroma
  • Flavor
  • Terpene profile
  • Cannabinoid composition
  • Product format
  • Individual effects

For some consumers, extremely high-potency products may not be desirable.

The best product is the one that fits the individual’s preferences, experience, and intended use.

What Are Terpenes?

If you’ve looked at cannabis products, you’ve probably also seen the word terpenes.

Terpenes are aromatic compounds found in many plants, including cannabis.

They contribute to the smell and flavor of cannabis.

Different cannabis varieties can contain different terpene profiles, which is one reason products can smell and taste very different from one another.

Some commonly discussed cannabis terpenes include:

  • Myrcene
  • Limonene
  • Pinene
  • Caryophyllene
  • Linalool

Terpenes don’t determine a product’s effects by themselves, but they are an important part of the overall characteristics of cannabis.

Understanding Edible Labels

Edibles require particular attention to the label.

An edible package might contain a certain total amount of THC, but that amount may be divided into multiple servings.

For example, a package could contain:

100 mg total THC

with:

10 servings × 10 mg THC

Those are very different numbers when considering an individual serving.

Always check both:

Total THC per package

and

THC per serving.

If you’re new to edibles, follow the product’s labeled serving instructions and avoid taking additional servings simply because you don’t feel effects immediately.

Edible effects can take longer to appear than inhaled cannabis.

Start Low and Go Slow

If you’re inexperienced with cannabis, potency deserves attention.

Everyone responds differently, and factors such as tolerance, product type, consumption method, and individual physiology can influence the experience.

For products such as edibles, it can take significantly longer for effects to become noticeable.

Following the labeled serving information and giving the product adequate time before consuming more can help avoid accidentally consuming more than intended.

Cannabis Can Affect Everyone Differently

There’s no universal cannabis experience.

One person might describe a product as relaxing, while another person may have a very different experience.

Your response can be influenced by factors including:

  • Individual tolerance
  • Product potency
  • Consumption method
  • Amount consumed
  • Previous cannabis experience
  • Other substances or medications
  • Individual physiology

That’s why responsible consumption and paying attention to product labeling are important.

Read the Entire Product Label

Before purchasing or consuming a cannabis product, look beyond the front of the package.

Check for:

  • THC content
  • CBD content
  • Serving size
  • Total servings
  • Ingredients
  • Product type
  • Manufacturer information
  • Storage instructions
  • Warnings
  • Recommended serving information

The more familiar you become with these details, the easier it becomes to compare products.

Choosing Between THC, CBD, or Both

So, should you choose THC, CBD, or a combination?

There’s no single answer.

It depends on what type of product you’re interested in, your experience level, your personal preferences, and the product’s labeled characteristics.

If you’re unsure, speak with the dispensary team.

They can help explain the differences between products and point you toward options that match what you’re looking for.

Knowledge Makes Shopping Easier

Understanding THC and CBD doesn’t mean you need to become a cannabis expert overnight.

Start with the basics.

Know what THC and CBD are. Learn how to read potency and serving information. Pay attention to product type and ingredients. And don’t hesitate to ask questions.

The more comfortable you become with cannabis terminology, the easier it becomes to make informed decisions.
